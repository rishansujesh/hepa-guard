# AASLD MASLD Decision Tree (2023) — Transcribed (for RAG)

> Source: AASLD “2023 MASLD Decision Tree — Explanations At A Glance” (figure).
> Note: This is a text transcription of a figure to enable retrieval + citation. Multiple etiologies may coexist; exclude other causes as appropriate.

## Start Here: Initial screening trigger
### 1) Does the patient have elevated liver enzymes?
- ALT > 33 (males)
- ALT > 25 (females)

If **YES** → go to **2**  
If **NO** → go to **3**

---

## 2) If elevated ALT: rule out other chronic liver disease causes
**Have you ruled out Hepatitis B, C or other causes of chronic liver disease?**

Suggested workup (examples from the figure):
- Hepatitis C Ab with reflex to PCR
- Hepatitis B: sAg, sAb, cAb
- Ceruloplasmin
- Immunoglobulins
- ANA, ASMA, AMA
- Iron studies
- Celiac antibodies
- Alpha-1-AT levels
- OR rule out other underlying etiologies of liver disease

If **YES** → proceed to **3**  
If **NO** → complete the above evaluation (rule out other causes) and then proceed.

---

## 3) Does the patient have hepatic steatosis?
As identified by:
- Imaging
- Biopsy

If **NO** → **No, it is likely not Metabolic Dysfunction–Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD).**  
If **YES** → **The patient has steatotic liver disease (SLD).** Continue to **4**.

---

## 4) Are there other causes of steatosis?
Examples:
- Alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD)
- Drug-induced liver injury (DILI)
- Monogenic diseases
- Miscellaneous (see SLD classifications)

If **YES** → **Investigate for other underlying liver etiologies.**  
If **NO** (or after evaluation) → continue to **5**.

---

## 5) Does the adult patient meet any ONE cardiometabolic criterion?
If **NO** → **Your patient may have cryptogenic SLD or possible MASLD.**  
If **YES** → continue to alcohol assessment (**6**).

### Adult cardiometabolic criteria (at least 1 out of 5)
1. **BMI ≥ 25 kg/m²** *(≥ 23 kg/m² in Asia)*  
   **OR** waist circumference > 94 cm (M) or > 80 cm (F) *(or ethnicity-adjusted thresholds)*
2. **Fasting serum glucose ≥ 5.6 mmol/L (100 mg/dL)**  
   **OR** 2-hour post-load glucose ≥ 7.8 mmol/L (≥ 140 mg/dL)  
   **OR** HbA1c ≥ 5.7% (39 mmol/L)  
   **OR** type 2 diabetes / treatment for type 2 diabetes
3. **Blood pressure ≥ 130/85 mmHg** OR specific antihypertensive drug treatment
4. **Plasma triglycerides ≥ 1.70 mmol/L (150 mg/dL)** OR lipid-lowering treatment
5. **Plasma HDL-cholesterol ≤ 1.0 mmol/L (40 mg/dL) (M)** and **≤ 1.3 mmol/L (50 mg/dL) (F)**  
   OR lipid-lowering treatment

---

## 6) Alcohol assessment (to distinguish MASLD vs MetALD vs primarily ALD)
### 6A) Does your patient regularly drink >50 g/day (females) or >60 g/day (males)?
If **YES** → **Your patient has primarily ALD.**  
If **NO** → continue to **6B**.

### 6B) Does your patient regularly drink alcohol in the “MetALD range”?
- **Females:** 140–350 g/week (20–50 g/day)
- **Males:** 210–420 g/week (30–60 g/day)

If **YES** → **Your patient has Met-ALD.**  
If **NO** → **Your patient has MASLD.**

> Note: Multiple etiologies may coexist in a patient; exclude other causes of liver disease as appropriate.

---

## 7) Cirrhosis screen (when SLD + cardiometabolic criteria present)
**Does the patient have features suggestive of cirrhosis?**

If **YES** → **Your patient may have Metabolic Dysfunction–Associated Steatotic Liver Disease cirrhosis.**  
- We also encourage checking for other discernible causes.

If **NO** → continue management per practice guidance.
